E2 design-based validation: notes (generated by e2_design_based.py)
========================================================================
Data: replication/data/acs/acs2023_households.parquet (ACS 2023 1-year PUMS,
  occupied units with HINCP > 0), 1,326,716 households, 2,462 PUMAs, 51 states/DC.
  Treated as a census; weights wgtp; y = adjusted HINCP (2023 dollars).
  Population weighted median of y = 79,522.40; z = 0.6 x median = 47,713.44.
  National weighted Gini of y = 0.4739.

Design actually used
  Replications (main exercise): 10 (seeds SEED_ROOT + 10000 r, SEED_ROOT = 20260601).
  Reduced from the target design (main 20, coverage 10) for run time: main 10, coverage 5.
  Sampling stage 1: 20% of PUMAs within each state, rounded, at least 1 per state,
    drawn uniformly without replacement.
  Sampling stage 2: 30 households per sampled PUMA, probability proportional to wgtp,
    without replacement (numpy choice with p). Training fits are unweighted.
  Target population: all households in non-sampled PUMAs (about 80% of the population).
  Predictor regimes: unit = all u_* columns (34); context = all c_* columns (13),
    constant within PUMA.
  Models: lgbm (estimators.make_model 'lgbm', main) and ols (LinearRegression), on log y.
  Estimators: N, C1 (linear recalibration), C2 (pooled residual simulation), C3 (quantile
    regression forest, 19 quantile levels 0.05-0.95, see deviations), C4 (decile residual
    bootstrap). R = 10 draws for C2, C3 and C4 in the main exercise.
    Why R = 10 and not 100: cost. Each draw is one pass over about 1.06M target households at
    three aggregation levels; R = 100 would have multiplied the run time by about 10 on this
    machine. The simulation variability of the averaged indices was not measured separately.
    Coverage uses R = 3 (task specification).
  Indices: Gini, MLD, Theil, FGT0, FGT1 (z fixed), weighted by wgtp.
  Truth: indices of actual y in the same target population of the same replication.
  Metrics: relative bias = mean over (units, reps) of (est - true)/true, in %;
    relative RMSE = sqrt(mean over (units, reps) of ((est - true)/true)^2), in %.
    Area level units are non-sampled PUMAs; state and national levels pool over states/reps.
    Spearman: rank correlation of estimated and true PUMA Gini across PUMAs, per rep, then
    averaged over reps. share_gini_below_puma: share of (PUMA, rep) pairs with est < true.
    Relative errors with a zero truth (none expected) are excluded.

Cross-fitting: estimators.py uses 5-fold random KFold on the training rows (unchanged).
Estimates use predict_welfare_draws (added to estimators.py); it follows estimate_indices
  step by step, with the same random-number order (existing functions unchanged).

Coverage (bootstrap)
  Replications: [0, 1, 2, 3, 4] (B = 30, R = 3).
  PUMA-cluster bootstrap: sampled PUMAs resampled with replacement; training households of
  a duplicated PUMA are duplicated; cross-fitting folds are assigned per household so
  that copies of the same household share a fold. Percentile 5-95 interval.
  The bootstrap resamples the training sample only: the target population of each rep is
  held fixed, so intervals are conditional on the set of non-sampled PUMAs.
  Coverage run time: 64.8 min total.

Deviations and limitations
  - C3 uses 19 quantile levels (0.05, 0.10, ..., 0.95) instead of the 99 levels of
    estimators.fit_qrf_quantiles, with linear interpolation in probability between grid points
    and constant values beyond 0.05 and 0.95. Reason: predicting 99 quantiles for about 1.06M
    target households took several minutes per replication on the shared machine. The tails
    below the 5th and above the 95th percentile are therefore not represented. C3 inequality is
    likely biased downward relative to a full 99-point forest for this reason; the size of that
    effect was not measured.
  - LightGBM runs with 2 threads; estimators.make_model specifies 4. The script sets the thread
    count at run time, because a second job shared the 4 cores and 4-thread fits took 10-90 s
    instead of under 1 s. The model is the same; floating-point results may differ in the last digits.
  - The quantile forest also uses 2 threads, with the settings of fit_qrf_quantiles.
  - predict_welfare_draws (added to estimators.py) returns predicted welfare draws instead of
    aggregate indices, because area-level indices need each household's predicted welfare.
    estimate_indices itself is not called in the experiment.
  - Population is the 1.33M occupied, positive-income households of the 2023 1-year file
    (not the full 2023 PUMS with group quarters). Households are the unit; no person weights.
  - Context features are PUMA means of census variables, as in the design, not satellite data.
  - Area-level Spearman is computed on PUMAs; NaN if the estimate is constant (N, C1 in context).
  - Target PUMAs are fixed per replication by the sample; truth varies across replications.
